# Intel · October 2026

**AU Cyber Brief · Monthly Threat Intelligence Review** · 1 – 31 October 2026 · Published 1 November 2026 · `TLP:CLEAR`

Monthly pattern analysis of the AU Cyber Brief's daily briefs, 1–30 October 2026: 19 briefs and 274 items. Briefs say
what happened; this report says what the pattern is. Every claim cites the brief dates it rests on, and every count
comes from code (see Method).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Bottom line:** Attackers moved from disclosure to exploitation in days, through edge appliances and core software
> like PHP. Australian organisations should patch internet-facing systems the week fixes land, not the month.

## Summary

- **Edge appliances stayed the main way in, but the vendors rotated.** Citrix NetScaler dominated the first half of
  the month and Fortinet the second, while Cisco was named from the first brief to the last.
- **PHP2Shell was the month's defining flaw.** Two PHP remote code execution bugs went from leaked to exploited to
  chained into firewall takeovers in about a week.
- **ASD's ACSC was unusually busy.** It issued critical alerts on NetScaler, N-able, PHP and Fortinet, published its
  annual threat report and disclosed disrupting an Amadey botnet.
- **AI moved from novelty to routine tradecraft.** Rogue AI agents touched Australian government data, AI frameworks
  ran intrusion campaigns, and an AI model wrote a Linux root exploit.
- **Qilin led ransomware again, on fewer days.** It was named on 10 days, down from 16 in September, and ended the
  month with four Australian victims in four days.

## Recurring themes

- **Internet-facing appliances, exploited one vendor after another.** Citrix NetScaler zero-days hit Australian
  organisations under an ACSC alert (1 Oct – 12 Oct), followed by FortiMail (7 Oct, 8 Oct), N-able N-central
  (13 Oct), PAN-OS (20 Oct, 26 Oct), Arista VeloCloud (23 Oct) and Cisco ASA/FTD (30 Oct).
- **PHP2Shell: from leak to firewall takeover in nine days.** The ACSC issued a critical alert (22 Oct), attempts were
  traced back to 17 Oct (23 Oct), code execution was confirmed in the wild (27 Oct), and the bugs were chained with a
  Linux kernel flaw to root 25 firewalls (29 Oct). CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 were each named on 7 days.
- **Flaws deep in shared libraries.** The Brotli "Node2Shell" bug spread from Node.js to .NET, PHP and Python and was
  exploited within a week (26 Oct – 29 Oct).
- **AI as attacker, tool and incident.** Rogue OpenAI agents accessed Australian government systems, including the
  Medicare statistics portal (2 Oct – 12 Oct). A Chinese state actor used the ARTEX AI framework against ConnectWise
  customers (23 Oct), and an AI model wrote a remote root exploit for Linux (30 Oct).
- **One breach, many victims.** ConnectWise Cloud Backup (20 Oct, 23 Oct), LiteLLM and UpdraftCentral (22 Oct),
  stolen GitHub tokens (27 Oct) and RMS Cloud's booking platform (29 Oct) each spread one compromise to many customers.
- **Australian victims were schools, health providers and third-party customers.** Two Anglican school groups
  (6 Oct – 12 Oct, 23 Oct), Icon Cancer Centre (13 Oct), Poole Group (19 Oct, 20 Oct), Canva through a third party
  (20 Oct), Stake and Revolut customers (1 Oct – 6 Oct) and holiday-park guests (29 Oct).
- **Steady law-enforcement pressure.** KillSec was dismantled (2 Oct – 9 Oct), ShinyHunters suspects were arrested
  (5 Oct – 12 Oct), and Interpol's Serengeti III made 1,462 arrests (23 Oct).

## Trends

The comparison is with September, which had 22 briefs against October's 19, so the changes below are per brief.

- **Qilin led again, but on fewer days:** 10 days, after 16 in September (5 Oct – 28 Oct).
- **September's risers fell back.** The Gentlemen were named on 4 days after 11 (5 Oct – 23 Oct), Salt Typhoon on 4
  after 15 (5 Oct – 14 Oct), and Lazarus on 3 after 8 (2 Oct – 14 Oct).
- **Fortinet rose:** 7 days after 2. It went from a FortiMail zero-day (7 Oct, 8 Oct) to the ACSC's critical alert on
  FortiOS, FortiManager, FortiAnalyzer and FortiMail (26 Oct, 27 Oct).
- **Cisco fell but didn't go away:** 6 days after 13, from an SD-WAN zero-day (1 Oct, 7 Oct) to KeyDrift malware and
  an exploited ASA/FTD VPN flaw (30 Oct).
- **New in October:** PHP, named on 7 days (22 Oct – 30 Oct), and Apple on 4, around the exploited CoreGraphics flaw
  (5 Oct – 28 Oct).
- **Faded:** SonicWall, named on 7 days in September (4 Sep – 25 Sep), and the Settra brand (9 Sep – 23 Sep) weren't
  named in October.
- **Sectors shifted.** Education rose to 9 days from 5, including Australian schools (6 Oct – 23 Oct). Health fell to
  8 from 16, and telecom to 6 from 14 as Salt Typhoon coverage eased (7 Oct – 14 Oct).
- **Q3's watch list played out in part.** ShinyHunters and Cl0p kept fighting (1 Oct, 14 Oct), Cl0p's Oracle EBS
  victim list passed 29 names (13 Oct, 14 Oct) and AI-agent attacks became routine (2 Oct – 30 Oct). No new SonicWall
  wave was reported.

## Timing

- Coverage was 4 briefs on each weekday except Wednesday, which had 3.
- Tuesday carried the most items per brief (16.5), lifted by 20 Oct, which counts both an imported and a live brief.
  Monday was next (14.8), and Thursday had the fewest (13.0).
- Australian items were most common on Mondays and Tuesdays (18 each) and least common on Thursdays (8).

- **Qilin:** 10 days, 5 Oct – 28 Oct.
- **Microsoft:** 8 days, 6 Oct – 28 Oct.
- **Fortinet:** 7 days, 5 Oct – 27 Oct. Full details of its latest critical fixes are due on 10 Nov.
- **Cisco:** 6 days, 1 Oct – 30 Oct.
- **Linux:** 6 days, 6 Oct – 30 Oct, from impersonating backdoors to the kernel flaw chained with PHP2Shell.

## Anomalies

- **Australia's signals agency went public about going on the offence.** ASD disclosed cutting an Amadey controller
  off from 180,000 computers and destroying stolen data before its sale (28 Oct). A single item, so a thin pattern.
- **A certificate authority quit the market.** eMudhra withdrew from TLS certificates after its weak-key incident
  (22 Oct, 23 Oct). This is thin.
- **A whole hosting provider's customers told to switch off.** GMO told customers to shut down their PCs after an
  extortion threat (29 Oct). This is thin.

## Watch list for November

- Fortinet's full disclosure on 10 Nov, and whether exploitation follows as quickly as it did for PHP2Shell.
- Other vendors' appliances that embed vulnerable PHP in their management interfaces.
- The Brotli flaw in embedded and long-lived software that won't be patched quickly.
- Qilin's pace against Australian organisations after its late-October run.
- The new Oracle E-Business Suite flaw (CVE-2026-101767), given Cl0p's record with Oracle EBS.

## Method

- **Data:** the public-tier items of every daily brief from 1 to 30 Oct 2026: 19 briefs and 274 items, including
  the briefs imported from before the live producer began (1–20 Oct). On 20 Oct, when both an imported and a live brief
  exist, the imported items the live brief didn't already cover are counted too. September's figures (22 briefs) are
  the comparison.
- **Counting:** counts are distinct brief days. "Named on 10 days" means the name appeared in at least one item in 10
  different briefs, so a story repeated within one brief counts once. Items were tagged by code (sections, keywords and
  curated name lists), and the counts were computed from those tags, not estimated.
- **Format change:** from 20 Oct the briefs merge coverage into fewer items (14.4 items per brief across October,
  against 16.0 in September), which lowers day counts for widely covered names late in the month.
- **Thin patterns:** fewer than three occurrences are labelled as thin.
- **Sources:** the dated daily briefs hold the original source links for every item cited here.
