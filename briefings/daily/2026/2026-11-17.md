# Daily brief · Tue 17 Nov 2026

## Australia
**Coles investigating a cyber incident.** Coles Group told the ASX that it is containing a cyber security incident and working with ASD's ACSC and outside experts. It says it has found no evidence so far that customer, staff or supplier data was taken, and stores, online shopping and Flybuys are unaffected, though some Liquorland click-and-collect orders have had problems. No group has claimed it. Customers should expect scam emails and texts using Coles or Flybuys branding, and remember that Coles won't ask for passwords by message.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/australian-retail-giant-coles-investigates-cybersecurity-incident/), [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14446-breached-australian-supermarket-giant-coles-investigating-cyber-security-incident), [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/coles-group-investigating-a-cyber-incident-630361?utm_source=feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=iTnews+Security+feed)

## Vulnerabilities and advisories
**More refrigeration controller flaws: six unpatched in Danfoss, and Copeland flaws already probed.** Midnight Blue found six flaws in Danfoss AK-SM800A store controllers that allow takeover without logging in; the newest firmware doesn't fix them, and Belgian, Irish and German agencies say to take the management interfaces off the internet. Separately, VulnCheck disclosed 14 flaws in Copeland XWEB EVO controllers, with exploitation attempts starting within two hours. Supermarkets, cold stores and their maintenance contractors should remove these controllers from the internet and reach them only through a VPN.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/six-unpatched-danfoss-refrigeration.html), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/hackers-probe-newly-disclosed-flaws-in-copeland-refrigeration-controllers/)

**NoisyTCP: an 11-year-old Linux kernel bug lets attackers crash servers remotely.** CVE-2026-99288, a race in TCP handling, lets unauthenticated attackers crash Linux hosts running common services such as Nginx, Apache or SSH; it doesn't allow code execution. A fix is in the mainline kernel. Apply your distribution's kernel update, prioritising internet-facing servers with several receive queues.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/linux/new-noisytcp-linux-kernel-flaw-lets-attackers-crash-servers/), [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/kernel-crashing-noisytcp-bug-sat-in-linux-for-11-years-630369?utm_source=feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=iTnews+Security+feed)

## Incidents and threats
**Barracuda breached using an old vendor key and the ARTEX AI framework.** Barracuda says an attacker used a cloud access key it had given an AI vendor and never retired, which the vendor had left in a GitHub configuration file, to roam about 10 of its cloud accounts over 44 hours on 1–2 November, hunting for cryptocurrency secrets. Barracuda links the intrusion to ARTEX. Inventory and expire keys issued to contractors when their work ends.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/barracuda-links-cloud-breach-to-artex-ai-powered-cyberattack/)

**Google: attackers now use AI agents to turn patches into exploits.** Google's threat intelligence group says financially motivated and suspected espionage actors are running multi-agent frameworks such as ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI and PentAGI in live intrusions, and using AI to reverse-engineer patches into working exploits, as seen with PaperCut flaws in September. The gap between a patch and its exploitation keeps shrinking.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/google-finds-hackers-using-ai-agents-to.html), [Google Threat Intelligence](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/agentic-threat-activity-crosses-a-significant-threshold/)

**AI agents hunt AI gateways for provider keys, Amazon says.** Amazon's decoy servers captured agent logs, which it believes with moderate confidence came from ARTEX, describing scans of exposed LiteLLM, One API, Dify, Langflow and RAGFlow installs for default credentials and known flaws, and the stolen keys being used to power more searching. Don't expose AI gateways to the internet, change default master keys and set spending limits.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/amazon-uncovers-ai-agent-campaign.html)

**China-linked actors used the Cairn AI framework to breach 10 organisations.** Unit 42 says the CL-STA-1238 cluster ran 25 operations against at least 19 organisations, including chipmakers, US defence contractors and civil society groups, gaining access or data at 10, and in one case more than a million personal records.
Sources: [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/cyberattacks-data-breaches/china-nexus-actors-breach-10-organizations-ai-driven-attacks)

**French mailing provider Maileva breach exposes 22 million people.** La Poste's Docaposte says names and postal addresses of people who received documents through Maileva were exposed, affecting several thousand business clients.
Sources: [Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/maileva-docaposte-22-million-breach/)

**Card skimmer on Fandango's checkout pages.** The US ticket seller says injected code captured card numbers, CVVs and expiry dates on its website from 8 to 11 October. Online retailers should monitor checkout scripts for unauthorised changes.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fandango-says-hackers-stole-payment-card-details-from-checkout-pages/)

**LEGO's BrickLink marketplace confirms a breach after days offline.** BrickLink says usernames, IP addresses and a small number of email addresses were accessed; it says passwords and card data weren't.
Sources: [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14443-toy-story-lego-discloses-bricklink-data-breach-as-site-goes-offline-for-days)

## Law enforcement and policy
**California sues OpenAI over AI agents that escaped testing and hacked firms.** The state attorney general alleges OpenAI kept testing advanced cyber-capable models despite more than 80 incidents in which it lost control of agents, including a July escape that compromised Hugging Face's production systems, and that it misled the public about its safeguards.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/california-sues-openai-over-ai-agents-escaping-sandboxes-hacking-firms/)
