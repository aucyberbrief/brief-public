# Daily brief · Fri 6 Nov 2026

## Australia
**Victorian medical group Epichealth confirms patient data breach after The Gentlemen listing.** Epichealth told Cyber Daily that an unauthorised third party accessed part of its IT systems. Its patient notice says names, contact details, dates of birth, Medicare, Centrelink and DVA numbers and health information may be involved. The Gentlemen ransomware group listed the clinics on 31 October and threatened to publish data. Clinics remain open; the OAIC and ASD's ACSC have been told. Patients should watch for scams that quote their Medicare or Centrelink details.
Sources: [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14406-exclusive-epichealth-medical-clinics-confirms-ransomware-related-data-breach)

## Vulnerabilities and advisories
**OpenSSH emergency fix: long passwords open password-less accounts on Alpine Linux.** On musl-based systems such as Alpine, an oversized password can log in to accounts that have no password set, where sshd checks passwords without PAM and password logins are on (Alpine's default). Alpine's official cloud images can turn this into root through the default alpine user. OpenSSH 10.6p2 and Alpine's updated packages fix it; no attacks are reported. Update and restart sshd, or set PasswordAuthentication to no.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openssh-issues-emergency-fix-for-flaw.html), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/openssh-flaw-lets-attackers-log-into-password-disabled-linux-accounts/)

**CISA adds the Apple flaw used in the APT29-linked phone attacks to its exploited list.** CVE-2024-27834, a pointer authentication bypass Apple fixed in May 2024, joined the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after this week's reports of watering-hole attacks on iPhones. US agencies must patch by 18 November. Make sure no managed Apple devices are still on OS versions older than the 2024 fixes.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-patched-apple-flaw-to.html)

**Apache OFBiz fixes critical pre-authentication flaws.** The maintainers fixed 46 flaws, including CVE-2026-68524, which allows remote code execution without logging in, and CVE-2026-109928, which allows full account takeover. OFBiz has a history of rapid exploitation. Upgrade to 24.09.08.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/threatsday-apache-ofbiz-rce-dreamhost.html)

## Incidents and threats
**US agencies warn of an ongoing threat to refrigeration controllers.** CISA, the FBI and others say attackers are exploiting internet-accessible, unpatched temperature and refrigeration controllers to force heating and defrost cycles, after the attacks on European supermarkets and France's blood service. They say every organisation that relies on cold storage should assume it could be a target. Take controllers off the internet, separate them from corporate networks, use strong authentication and patch.
Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/cyberattacks-refrigeration-cisa-fbi), [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/us-warns-of-ongoing-cyber-threat-to-industrial-refrigeration-systems-630152?utm_source=feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=iTnews+Security+feed)

**DigiCert breach exposes customer contact data and some identity documents.** A 2025 server migration left an internal business system reachable from the internet, and an authorisation flaw let a self-registered account read archived customer records in mid-2026. Data included names, business contacts and, for some customers, driver's licences, passports or utility bills. DigiCert says certificate issuance and customer logins weren't affected. Customers who supplied identity documents for validation should watch for targeted phishing.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/digicert-data-breach-exposes-customer-information-identity-documents/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/threatsday-apache-ofbiz-rce-dreamhost.html)

**Chainalysis: names, password hashes and live tokens of all users exposed.** The blockchain analytics firm, whose customers include law enforcement and national security agencies, widened its 30 October breach disclosure. It says it still has no evidence investigations data was accessed. All users must reset passwords and rotate API keys, going to the login page directly rather than through emailed links.
Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/chainalysis-all-users-hit-by-agentic-cyberattack)

**DreamHost breach exposed FTP and SFTP logins for two months.** Attackers exploited a flaw in DreamHost's web File Manager from August to 23 October, taking FTP/SFTP credentials of anyone who used it, and may have accessed some webmail sessions. Customers should reset those passwords and check their sites for unexpected files.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/dreamhost-confirms-data-breach-exposed-ftp-logins-and-webmail-sessions/)

**Trojanized Control Web Panel server backdoors Linux hosts.** XLab found CWPdoor, a stealthy passive backdoor that replaces CWP's cwpsrv web server and hides its trigger requests from logs; more than 340,000 CWP instances are exposed online. Hosting providers should check for the backdoor's helper socket and processes, and if found, replace the binary, upgrade and rotate panel credentials.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-backdoor-linux-servers-using-trojanized-cwp-web-server/)

**Stolen Entra app credentials used to reach MSP customers.** Todyl says an unknown actor used stolen Microsoft Entra ID application credentials and Azure DevOps identities to get into managed service providers' Microsoft 365 and Azure tenants, reaching at least 27 organisations including government and energy clients. MSPs should review app registrations, rotate client secrets and check service principal sign-ins.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/threatsday-apache-ofbiz-rce-dreamhost.html)

## Research
**OpenAI's AI pipeline produced 948 Linux root exploits in one run.** Using an unreleased cyber model, OpenAI says it reproduced 1,724 Linux kernel flaws and built 948 working privilege-escalation exploits in about 30 hours for US$1.2 million, with patches reviewed by humans before reporting. It says a low-privileged account on an unpatched system should now be treated as equivalent to root for a well-resourced attacker. Keep kernels current and limit who has shell access.
Sources: [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/low-privileged-accounts-on-unpatched-systems-as-good-as-root-openai-630145?utm_source=feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=iTnews+Security+feed), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openai-ai-pipeline-generates-948-linux.html)

It's the third round of sanctions on the group.
