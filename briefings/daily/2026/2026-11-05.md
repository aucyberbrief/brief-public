# Daily brief · Thu 5 Nov 2026

## Australia
**NSW Health server breach declared a major incident.** Cyber Security NSW says an isolated NSW Health server was breached and the NSW Police Cybercrime Squad is investigating. Police were alerted on 30 October. It says no patient data has been affected or is currently at risk; a hacker claims on a criminal forum to have taken data from a misconfigured subdomain. Health providers should check internet-facing subdomains for misconfigurations and stale servers.
Sources: [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/nsw-health-server-targeted-in-cyber-incident-630096?utm_source=feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=iTnews+Security+feed), [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14396-breached-nsw-police-investigating-major-cyber-security-incident-impacting-nsw-health)

**BYOD ransomware group claims IHG Hotels & Resorts, which runs 69 hotels in Australia.** The group behind recent attacks on the University of New Mexico and Michigan State listed the hotel group, owner of brands including InterContinental, Crowne Plaza and Holiday Inn, on 3 November. It hasn't published evidence, and IHG hadn't responded to Cyber Daily. Guests of IHG hotels should watch for phishing that quotes real bookings.
Sources: [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14399-exclusive-dozens-of-australian-hotels-potentially-impacted-by-alleged-ihg-hotels-resorts-breach)

**OAIC won't pursue collapsed court transcriber over offshore access.** The privacy regulator found a reasonable basis for concern that VIQ Solutions breached the Privacy Act after staff of an Indian subcontractor accessed audio from 93 Federal Court and Federal Circuit and Family Court matters between September 2025 and February 2026. It is taking no further action because VIQ is in liquidation. Agencies outsourcing sensitive work should check where subcontractors actually operate.
Sources: [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/no-oaic-probe-of-collapsed-viq-over-court-transcription-work-in-india-630103?utm_source=feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=iTnews+Security+feed), [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14398-oaic-investigation-finds-limited-offshore-access-to-australian-court-recordings)

## Vulnerabilities and advisories
**Zyxel switch zero-day used to hack nearly 1,000 devices.** Zyxel has patched CVE-2026-107441 in GS1900 switches, an unauthenticated command execution flaw in the web interface that attackers used to steal configuration files and credentials. The exploit worked even against firmware patched for an earlier flaw. Update, take management interfaces off the internet, and rotate credentials stored on affected switches.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/zyxel-fixes-zero-day-exploited-to-hack-nearly-1-000-network-switches/)

**APT package manager flaw lets attackers swap packages on Debian and Ubuntu.** CVE-2026-109518 lets an attacker who can tamper with package downloads replace a verified package and run code as root, when APT retries failed downloads (the default from Debian 12 and Ubuntu 22.04). Debian has fixed APT and Ubuntu updates are rolling out. Update APT and prefer HTTPS mirrors.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/debian-and-ubuntu-apt-flaw-lets-mitm.html)

**Android November update fixes a critical zero-click flaw.** CVE-2026-58868 in Android 14 to 17 can allow remote code execution without user interaction; it's fixed in patch level 2026-11-01. No exploitation is reported. Push the update to managed devices as phone makers release it.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/google-patches-critical-android-flaw.html)

**Critical Rails flaw lets crafted uploads write files on the server.** Apps that process uploads with Active Storage and ImageMagick can be made to write, and in some setups read, arbitrary files, exposing secrets such as secret_key_base. Upgrade to the latest Rails 8.1 or 8.0 patch release, or restrict ImageMagick's policy.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/critical-rails-flaw-lets-attackers.html)

**Xen flaws let one guest VM read and write other VMs' memory.** CVE-2026-102703 and CVE-2026-102704 (XSA-523) affect Xen 4.6 onward, including access to dom0, and have no mitigation. Hosting providers and anyone running multi-tenant Xen should patch now.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/xen-hypervisor-flaws-let-one-malicious.html)

## Incidents and threats
**Refrigeration attacks spread to France's blood service.** France's national blood service says an attack on a refrigeration equipment provider changed temperature and defrost settings at three facilities on 2 November, threatening stored blood. It follows this week's attacks on supermarket refrigeration across Europe; earlier attacks used known flaws in Danfoss controllers. Anyone running remotely managed cold storage should check exposure and vendor access now.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cyberattack-disrupts-blood-storage-refrigeration-systems-in-france/), [The Record](https://therecord.media/france-cyberattack-blood-refrigeration), [Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/france-blood-service-cyberattack/)

**APT29-linked watering holes target unpatched iPhones and Android phones.** Sekoia says Russia's APT29 compromised websites and a Ukrainian web analytics script to fingerprint visitors and serve known iOS and Android exploit chains that steal cookies and device data. Keep phones on current OS versions; these exploits target older, already-patched flaws.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/apt29-linked-hackers-hijack-web.html), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-use-n-day-exploits-in-zero-click-attacks-on-ios-android-devices/), [Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/apt29-watering-holes-iphones/)

**French bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa confirms theft through a supplier.** The bank says attackers compromised an unnamed supplier and made transfers from the bank's own account, with an estimated loss of about US$133 million; customer accounts weren't affected. Review what payment access your suppliers hold.
Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/french-bank-confirms-cyber-heist)
