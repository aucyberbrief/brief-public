# Daily brief · Tue 3 Nov 2026

## Australia
**Google dismantles a 1.3-million-device Android TV box botnet that reached into Australia.** Google says Shenzhen-based maker Videostrong deliberately built a backdoor, x.cpe, into its Android TV boxes and other devices and sold access to criminals for ad fraud and residential proxies. Google took down the botnet's control servers in late October and banned Videostrong from certified Android. Trend Micro's TrendAI counted infected devices in 206 models, with Australia among the countries hit (about 26,000 to 42,000 devices each in the US, Germany, the UK, Australia and Canada). The backdoor stays on the boxes and owners can't remove it, so take uncertified Android TV boxes off home and business networks.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-disrupts-million-device-xcpe-android-malware-botnet-bans-tv-box-maker/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/google-disrupts-13-million-device.html), [Google Threat Intelligence](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/shutting-the-door-on-the-xcpe-botnet/)

**Australian organisations report AI threats outpacing staff training.** ESET's survey of 400 Australian security decision-makers found two-thirds faced at least one AI-related threat in the past year, a third had data leak through AI platforms, and 72% had at least one major incident. Half say staff awareness is a leading challenge even though almost all train employees at least yearly. ESET recommends more realistic, regular phishing simulations.
Sources: [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14380-report-training-and-cyber-skills-gaps-continue-to-grow-as-ai-threats-increase)

## Vulnerabilities and advisories
**More than 1,000 GitLab servers still unpatched against an exploited RCE flaw.** CVE-2026-97229, the maximum-severity CI/CD regex flaw that attackers are exploiting, remains unpatched on over 1,000 internet-facing self-managed GitLab servers. Upgrade now, and if you build GitLab on your own Ruby, update Ruby too.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/over-1-000-gitlab-servers-vulnerable-to-code-execution-attacks/)

**Synology warns of C3RB3R ransomware hitting outdated NAS devices.** Attackers are exploiting a DiskStation Manager flaw patched in late 2024 to encrypt data on NAS units that were never updated. Update DSM, turn on automatic updates, and keep NAS management off the internet.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/synology-warns-customers-of-ongoing-c3rb3r-ransomware-attacks/)

**Critical Langflow flaws allow unauthenticated takeover of AI workflow servers.** IBM disclosed two critical flaws (CVE-2026-93680, an authentication bypass, and CVE-2026-110186, code execution) in every Langflow release from 1.0.0, with a public Metasploit module already citing the bypass. Upgrade to 1.13.0, turn off automatic login and don't expose Langflow to the internet.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/critical-langflow-flaws-could-let.html)

**BTCPay Server hot wallets drained on outdated instances.** The payment processor's maintainers say attackers are stealing Bitcoin and Litecoin from hot wallets on older versions. Merchants self-hosting BTCPay should update immediately and check for unexpected payments.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/btcpay-server-warns-of-hot-wallet-thefts-targeting-outdated-instances/)

## Incidents and threats
**Hackers tamper with supermarket fridges across Europe.** Attackers remotely changed temperature settings, shut down freezers or triggered defrosting at more than 115 stores in Belgium, the Netherlands, Sweden, Luxembourg, Ireland and Estonia, causing stock losses. It follows earlier attacks on refrigeration controllers in October. Retailers using internet-connected refrigeration controllers should check they aren't exposed and change default credentials.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-disrupt-refrigeration-systems-at-european-supermarkets/), [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/new-cyberattack-on-supermarket-fridges-sweeps-across-europe-630074?utm_source=feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=iTnews+Security+feed)

**Apple sends mercenary spyware warnings to users in 99 countries.** Apple's latest threat notifications went to targets including journalists. Recipients should turn on Lockdown Mode and seek expert help.
Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/apple-warns-users-99-countries-mercenary-spyware)

**BYOD ransomware gang claims Michigan State University.** The group behind last week's University of New Mexico hospital attack listed the university, claiming data on 1.3 million students. The university links it to a credential compromise in early October that forced password resets for all accounts.
Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/michigan-state-university-byod-gang-cyber-incident)

## Research
**"LLMJacking" runs up six-figure cloud bills in a day.** Mandiant reports attackers hijacking organisations' access to cloud AI models, with fraudulent charges above US$100,000 in a single day and up to US$1 million in total at some victims, including in Asia-Pacific. Set spending alerts and quotas on AI services and treat model API keys as high-value secrets.
Sources: [Google Threat Intelligence](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/llmjacking-monetizing-compromised-cloud-models/)
