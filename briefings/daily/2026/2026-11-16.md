# Daily brief · Mon 16 Nov 2026

## Australia
**WA buyer loses nearly $200,000 to property settlement fraud.** WA ScamNet says scammers intercepted emails between a buyer and their mortgage broker, impersonated the broker and redirected $199,830 of settlement funds to a mule account. It's one of three such cases in Western Australia this year, with losses above $330,000, and eight settlement agents have reported clients being targeted. Buyers should confirm bank details by phone on a known number before any settlement payment, and conveyancers and brokers should warn clients that they never change bank details by email.
Sources: [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14438-property-scam-sees-wa-buyers-lose-200k-in-settlement-fraud)

**tcadarwin listed by HYFLOCK.** The HYFLOCK ransomware group has listed tcadarwin (tcadarwin.com.au) on its leak site, and both ransomware.live and RansomLook now show the listing. The organisation hasn't commented publicly.
Sources: [ransomware.live](https://www.ransomware.live/id/dGNhZGFyd2luQEhZRkxPQ0s=), [RansomLook](https://www.ransomlook.io//group/hyflock)

## Vulnerabilities and advisories
**Check Point fixes the gateway zero-day used with PHP2Shell.** Check Point has released hotfixes for CVE-2026-123039, a flaw in the Gaia OS DynamicCLI daemon that lets a local user run commands as root. It says the flaw was exploited against a couple of dozen customers, after attackers got in through PHP2Shell on Mobile Access portals; CISA has added it to its exploited list. Install the hotfix, or stop the dynamic_server process until you can. Then check for commands run outside the normal Clish wrapper, which suggest root access through the socket.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/check-point-patches-zero-day-that-lets-hackers-get-root-access/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-php2shell-flaws-in.html), [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/11/13/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

**ScreenConnect attacks traced to a blank-key flaw in the RMM+ extension; worm-like SCKit found.** Huntress says the attackers got into MSP ScreenConnect instances through RMM+ versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32, which accepted an empty access key when none had been set, letting commands run without login or MFA. Microsoft and others describe SCKit, a toolkit built to jump from infected customer devices to the technicians who connect to them, using an older ScreenConnect client flaw (CVE-2026-84869); it failed against patched clients. MSPs should remove or update RMM+, set an access key, keep technician clients patched and review Run Command history.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-worm-like-sckit-malware-targets-msps-in-screenconnect-attacks/), [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/13/sckit-abuses-screenconnect-deploy-remote-access-target-technician-sessions/)

**UnrealIRCd flaw exploited a day after its fix.** CVE-2026-114713 lets any user on a network of linked servers inject commands that run with server authority; the maintainer calls it the most critical UnrealIRCd issue in 15 years. CISA has added it to its exploited list. Update UnrealIRCd or apply the maintainer's spamfilter workaround.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-exploit-unrealircd-flaw-that.html)

**PostgreSQL fixes 52 flaws, including one that lets a database user run code.** CVE-2026-6460, a 22-year-old type confusion bug, lets a user who can create objects run code as the PostgreSQL operating-system user; a researcher has described a working exploit. Upgrade to 18.7, 17.12, 16.16, 15.20 or 14.25 and rebuild any C-language extensions.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/postgresql-patches-52-flaws-including.html)

## Incidents and threats
**FBI warns the ARTEX AI framework is driving attacks worldwide.** An FBI flash alert says several threat actors have used the open-source ARTEX framework since August, against finance, government, defence, health and technology targets, sometimes going from first contact to privileged access in under three hours. It plugs into commercial and open models and is good at finding overlooked systems such as partner portals. The FBI advises locking down internet-facing apps and cloud admin interfaces, rate-limiting sensitive APIs and alerting on bursts of automated requests.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fbi-warns-artex-ai-hacking-tool-is-powering-global-cyberattacks/), [The Record](https://therecord.media/artex-ai-tool-fbi-flash-alert), [Cisco Talos](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/artex-and-beyond/)

**Cursor breach, via a stolen Fireworks AI key, put a trojanised Claude Code extension in its marketplace.** Cursor says attackers used an access key taken from Fireworks AI to copy old coding-agent traces, some holding unredacted credentials, then got into its systems and published malicious versions of Anthropic's Claude Code extension on 29 October. Up to 5,000 developers may have installed it, which adds persistent SSH backdoors. Anyone who installed Claude Code from Cursor's marketplace around then should check authorized_keys, rebuild if affected and rotate credentials shared with Cursor.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cursor-confirms-security-breach-led-to-malicious-claude-code-extension/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/stolen-fireworks-ai-key-led-to-cursor.html)

**Phishers exploit last week's Microsoft 365 outage.** Microsoft saw fake service health notices citing the real Exchange Online incident and claiming held emails would be deleted within 24 hours unless users clicked to restore them. Tell staff that Microsoft never asks them to act on outage emails, and search mail logs for "Outage -" subject lines.
Sources: [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/phishers-quick-to-exploit-extended-m365-outage-630347?utm_source=feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=iTnews+Security+feed)

**German drone maker hit through its own update server.** Quantum Systems, which supplies reconnaissance drones to Germany's military, confirmed a late-October attack; reports say a malicious update to its mission-planning software disrupted drone operations. It believes it was targeted for supporting Ukraine. Defence suppliers should verify the signing and integrity of software updates they distribute.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/german-military-drone-operations-disrupted-by-cyberattack/)

**TSMC employee data leaked from a cloud provider.** TSMC confirmed that current and former employees' data being auctioned online matched data held by one of its cloud service providers, and it says its own systems weren't breached.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/tsmc-employee-data-breach-linked-to-cloud-provider/)

**Trezor confirms recovery-phrase-stealing implants in wallets sold by one reseller.** Three Trezor Safe 5 wallets sold by Indonesian reseller CryptoBilis had an extra circuit board that captured what was shown or entered on screen and sent it out over cellular networks. Trezor has released a firmware fix. Buy hardware wallets only from the manufacturer or authorised resellers, and move funds off any device bought from CryptoBilis.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/cryptocurrency/trezor-confirms-crypto-stealing-implants-in-three-wallets-releases-fix/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/trezor-confirms-hidden-hardware.html)

## Law enforcement and policy
**US disrupts North Korea's deepfake crypto-theft campaign and moves to seize US$1.2 billion from the Binance theft.** The FBI says North Korea's "FaceStealer" scheme used recorded videos and deepfaked calls to steal more than US$400 million from crypto and Web3 workers over 18 months, and its infrastructure has been seized. The Justice Department also moved to seize and restrain more than US$1.2 billion stolen in the Binance hack. Treat unexpected video meetings that ask you to install software or "fix audio" as an attack.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/fbi-says-north-korean-hackers-stole.html), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/us-disrupts-north-korean-campaign-behind-400-million-crypto-theft/)

**Germany gives its spy agencies powers to hack and sabotage.** The Bundestag passed a law letting intelligence agencies hack foreign systems, disrupt adversaries' supply chains and take down servers run by hostile state-backed hackers.
Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/german-parliament-passes-law-giving-spy-agencies-hacking-power)
