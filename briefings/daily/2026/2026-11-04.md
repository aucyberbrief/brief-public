# Daily brief · Wed 4 Nov 2026

## Australia
**Qantas checks exposure to the Amadeus travel-system breach.** Qantas says Amadeus, which runs booking technology for about 141 airlines, told it of unauthorised access to one of its systems, contained in September. Qantas has notified the ACSC and the National Office of Cyber Security and says its own systems weren't affected. Cathay Pacific says about 53,000 bookings were exposed over access lasting from August 2023 to September 2026, mostly names, booking references and flight details but in some cases contacts, travel documents and frequent flyer numbers. Qantas customers should watch for phishing that quotes real bookings.
Sources: [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14385-qantas-customer-data-possibly-exposed-in-amadeus-hack), [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14391-qantas-compromise-how-many-warnings-does-australia-need-asks-acs-boss)

**Sydney auction house First State Auctions confirms a breach.** The luxury goods auctioneer confirmed an outsider accessed part of a system holding customer information after a hacker offered the data on a criminal forum. It says the data is mostly basic contact details, not card numbers or passwords, and it has told customers, the ACSC and the OAIC. Customers should expect targeted phishing.
Sources: [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/14393-exclusive-sydney-based-first-state-auctions-customer-data-impacted-by-cyber-incident)

## Vulnerabilities and advisories
**Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050 flaw exploited to install botnet malware.** VulnCheck says attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-58115 (CVSS 10), a missing-authentication flaw in the Node-RED interface on IoT2050 Advanced devices, to run code and pull down a botnet binary. Update to Siemens' fixed release, or remove or lock down Node-RED, and keep these devices off the internet.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-exploit-critical-siemens.html)

**Barracuda Email Security Gateway flaws can be chained to root.** Barracuda warns of a critical authentication flaw (CVE-2026-116606) that lets attackers change an appliance's configuration, and a command injection flaw (CVE-2026-111560) that runs commands as root. Both need access to the admin interface. Patch, and restrict admin access to trusted internal networks.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/barracuda-warns-of-critical-flaws-allowing-root-access-to-devices/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/barracuda-flaws-could-be-chained-to.html)

**Red Hat ships fixes for the 9.8-rated Linux TCP flaw.** Fixed kernels for CVE-2026-98432 are out for RHEL 8, 9 and 10, and Red Hat now rates it critical. Ubuntu's fixes are still only in its testing channel. Any listening TCP port can trigger it without authentication, so patch internet-facing Linux servers first.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/red-hat-patches-98-rated-linux-tcp-rce.html)

## Incidents and threats
**Security tool's update channel hijacked to backdoor Linux servers.** An attacker used stolen credentials to push a malicious script through cPGuard's updates on 2 November, installing a backdoor and copying password hashes and other sensitive files from customers' servers. Update to cPGuard 5.88.25 or later to remove it, then change passwords and keys on any server that may have run versions 5.88.12 to 5.88.19.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/cpguard-update-channel-hijacked-to.html)

**Binance: a fake job interview opened the door to the US$6.3 billion theft.** Binance's post-mortem says North Korean attackers posed as recruiters, had an engineer run a malicious "coding assignment" that endpoint tools didn't detect, and worked across several employees' devices over months. Binance says it stopped 57 other North Korean attempts this year. Treat take-home coding tests and recruiter approaches to developers as a live attack path.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/binance-says-fake-job-interview-led-to-63-billion-crypto-heist/), [The Record](https://therecord.media/binance-cryptocurrency-theft-months-long-campaign-nkorea), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/binance-reveals-how-fake-job-interviews.html)

**AI-driven intrusions reach more developer platforms.** WorkOS says an attacker using the ARTEX AI framework breached an internal sales app and took business contact records. Chainstack says an intruder reached production systems through a third-party component and accessed customer details and password hashes; The Hacker News links it to the same AI-powered campaign. Chainstack users should change their passwords.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/workos-says-attacker-used-artex-ai-to-breach-internal-sales-app/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/chainstack-reports-production-systems.html)

**mSecure password manager tells users to reset passwords, 2FA and passkeys.** The developer emailed current and former customers about suspicious activity without saying what happened or whether vaults were exposed. Users should follow the advice now rather than wait for details.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/msecure-warns-users-to-change-passwords.html)

**AI shows up inside ransomware intrusions.** Bitdefender says a 3AM ransomware affiliate that encrypted a UK school estate appears to have used an automated AI loop, re-issuing slightly modified commands every 40 to 100 seconds. The UK's NCSC expects AI-powered malware in ransomware attacks on its critical infrastructure by 2028.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/uk-school-estate-hit-by-3am-ransomware-in-suspected-ai-assisted-attack/), [NCSC UK](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/news/cyber-chief-uk-facing-ai-powered-attacks-on-critical-infrastructure-in-two-years), [Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/ncsc-ai-ransomware-threat-uk-cni/)
